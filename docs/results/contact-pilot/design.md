# Contact and language divergence • pilot design

Recorded before the pilot run. The experimental unit is an independently seeded
world. Each of 24 worlds (seeds 1–24) starts with six communities and 40 meanings.
A generation passes when the next cohort has learned a conventional word for
every meaning. Generations and contact rates are model choices, without a mapping
to Roman dates.

## Paired worlds

Begin with one shared vocabulary, introduce one local sound substitution per
region, and run 10 generations of sustained contact. Copy this state into three
branches. For 40 further generations, vary only where tutors come from. Then
restore sustained contact to all branches for 30 generations.

| Branch | Tutor outside the region during generations 11–50 |
|---|---|
| Sustained | 25% probability, uniformly among the five other regions |
| Fragmented | 5% probability, uniformly among immediate neighbors on a six-region chain |
| Isolated | 0% probability |

Every learner cohort receives one transmission for each meaning, including in
isolation. Branches share draws for tutor selection, sound change and independent
word replacement. Partial fragmentation changes both the frequency and reach of
contact. This pilot estimates their combined effect.

## Outcomes and decision

The primary outcome is the **fragmented minus sustained difference in cross-region
word comprehension at generation 50**. A difference of five percentage points
in either direction is the pilot's practical benchmark, chosen for interpretation.
Report every seed, the paired mean, and a 95% paired bootstrap interval (10,000
resamples of seed pairs). The interval describes simulation variability under these
rules. All other contrasts, including generation 80 after reconnection, are
descriptive. Plot each trajectory from the initial state through reconnection.

At each observation, test every meaning for every ordered pair of different
regions. The listener chooses the closest word in its own vocabulary by number of
differing sound positions. Ties split credit evenly. The test cannot teach, draw
on inventory, or see root identities. Report mean comprehension in both
directions combined, the fraction of same-meaning words sharing a root, and the
mean fraction of differing sound positions among those shared-root pairs.
Borrowing can transfer a root. The shared-root metric counts inherited and borrowed
matches. Inspect the isolation branch to see whether distinctive forms can develop
and the reconnection period to see which differences persist.

## Transmission rules

Each region has one conventional form per meaning. Forms contain four alternating
consonant and vowel positions (10 consonants, 5 vowels). A learner copies the
selected tutor's complete form and root from the previous generation. With
probability 0.004 per meaning, a new form with an independent root replaces it.
With probability 0.45 per generation, a region substitutes one sound throughout
its vocabulary with another sound of the same class. All draws are independent
of communicative success. There are no prescribed regional accents, trade payoffs,
or fitted Romance-language targets. These rules represent community conventions
and abstract sounds. They omit individual multilingualism, grammar, literacy,
and state institutions. The intervention models one possible consequence of
political fragmentation.
